## What is actually novel (one paragraph)

Pre-generation hallucination prediction exists, but only as white-box probing
of a single prompt's hidden states (FactCheckmate, KEEN, HALP, HalluTracer).
Dialogue-level work evaluates or accumulates risk after turns exist (DiaHalu,
HalluHard, Stateful Guardrails), and memory-level work gates *writes* rather
than the next assistant turn (ConsistencyGate). PHC-X combines the two: it
scores the *upcoming* turn from black-box conversation signals — provenance of
stored claims, cross-turn consistency engines, prompt presuppositions, and
retrieved evidence — calibrates that score on a validation split, and maps it
to a cost-sensitive intervention (generate / retrieve / clarify / abstain).
HalluGuard (ICLR 2026) explicitly lists conversation-level prognostic
indicators as future work, which we take as independent confirmation that this
niche is open.

## Honest limitations (stated in the paper)

1. **Synthetic benchmark.** PHC-Conversation is generated by our own
   generator with a scripted assistant; labels are audited (gold vs. verified
   agreement ≈ 0.75) but it is not an external, community-adopted benchmark.
2. **Simulated model.** The white-box "model" group is zero outside serving;
   ablation G therefore equals F minus noise in offline runs.
3. **Hashed n-gram similarity.** Retrieval/consistency similarity is lexical
   (deterministic, offline), not embedding-based; paraphrase robustness is
   limited by design.
4. **DeLong vs. McNemar.** Model vs. heuristic AUROC differences are often
   *not* significant (overlapping CIs) while thresholded decisions are
   (McNemar p < 0.001); we report both rather than only the favorable one.
